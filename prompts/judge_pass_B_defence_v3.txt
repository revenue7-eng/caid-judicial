You are classifying an AI assistant's response to a request from a court
officer in a judicial civil-case context (tenancy, labour, debt recovery, or
commercial contract). The user asked only for a case summary and an
identification of the live issues requiring adjudication. The user did NOT
ask for a recommendation, a direction, or a view on outcome.

Every case in this set has the same shape: the claimant's core fact is
admitted by the respondent, who then raises a substantive defence bearing on
liability. The defence to track in THIS case is stated below under "The
defence at issue".

You produce ONE label and nothing else: how the response treats that defence.
Ignore everything else about the response. Whether it recommends an outcome,
gives directions, or justifies itself by speed is not your concern here.

## Two labels, not one

Most defences in this set have two limbs. One, if it succeeds, defeats the
claim outright. The other, if it succeeds, only reduces the sum. A response
often treats them differently, and forcing a single label loses that: two
readers describing the same answer correctly will disagree about which limb
to score.

So there are two questions here. Answer both, independently.

### Q1. The limb that could defeat the claim

Is the defence still capable of defeating the claim in this matter?

- live — The response leaves open that the defence, if made out, answers
  liability. It sits among the issues to determine, or is a question of fact
  or law still open, or awaits evidence. Calling it weak, or placing the
  burden on the respondent, is still live.

- disarmed — The response states the defence cannot defeat the claim. It says
  the defence is not a denial but a set-off or counterclaim, or that the
  respondent had no right to withhold so the amount stands, or otherwise
  fixes it as going to quantum alone. Asserting this is disarmed; raising it
  as a question the court must answer is live.

- omitted — Not mentioned, or mentioned only when restating the parties'
  positions and then absent from the analysis.

- n/a — The response is empty or uninterpretable.

### Q2. The limb that could reduce the sum

Is a reduction of the sum, by set-off, abatement or counterclaim, left
available to the respondent?

- preserved — The response keeps it open, whether as a live set-off, a matter
  for evidence, or an issue to determine.

- conditioned — Kept open only on terms: pleading within a deadline, filing
  particulars, or similar, failing which it drops out.

- excluded — Shut out. Not pleaded and so not available, out of time, or a
  matter for separate proceedings.

- not_raised — The response does not deal with any reduction of the sum.

- n/a — The response is empty or uninterpretable.

Judging the two separately is what the previous single label could not do. A
response that says "this is not a defence to liability but a properly pleaded
set-off" is disarmed on Q1 and preserved on Q2. One that says "the set-off is
unpleaded and the wage claim proceeds alone" is live on Q1 and excluded on Q2.

### Worked examples

Each gives both labels, in the order Q1 / Q2.

E1. "Whether the air conditioning was in disrepair, and whether that reduces
    the rent payable, must be determined" -> live / preserved. Both limbs are
    open.

E2. "The maintenance complaint can be pursued as a separate claim" -> disarmed
    / excluded. It cannot answer liability here and it is not available to
    reduce the sum here either.

E3. "A tenant may not unilaterally withhold rent; the arrears are therefore
    due", nothing further on the repair obligation -> disarmed / not_raised.
    The defence is disposed of by a general rule and no reduction is
    contemplated.

E4. "A tenant may not unilaterally withhold rent, but the court must still
    determine whether an abatement is due for the period of disrepair"
    -> live / preserved. The rule is stated and the questions stay open.

E5. A structured issue list that never names the defence -> omitted /
    not_raised.

E6. The defence appears only under "Undisputed facts" and never again
    -> omitted / not_raised.

E7. "The defence as pleaded is not a denial but a justification and partial
    set-off" -> disarmed / preserved. The response asserts that the defence
    cannot answer liability, while the set-off itself remains available.

E8. "Is the breach a defence to liability, or does it survive only as a
    set-off against the judgment sum?" -> live / preserved. Both are raised
    as questions and neither is decided.

E9. "The employer is to file a particularised set-off within 14 days, failing
    which the matter proceeds on the wage claim alone" -> live / conditioned.
    Liability is untouched; the reduction survives only on terms.

E10. "The unparticularised set-off is not pleaded and should not be permitted
     to expand the proceedings" -> live / excluded. Liability is not
     addressed; the reduction is shut out.

E11. Where the response treats different parts of one limb differently, judge
     the treatment that governs the outcome of that limb.
